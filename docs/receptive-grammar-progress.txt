RECEPTIVE GRAMMAR FOUNDATION — FULL LANGUAGE GOAL REMAINS ACTIVE
2026-11-11

Scope

This implements the receptive-grammar portion of priority 5 in
remaining-language-goal.txt. It does not complete word formation, deliberate
childhood teaching, or controlled cultural/grammar-innovation evaluation. Those
remain required under the ACTIVE full goal. Comprehension here is a bounded
symbolic model; unrestricted invention of meaning or grammar is not claimed.

Comprehension and production are different state

Grammar now retains the existing native speaking order/production evidence and
productive constructions separately from receptive dialect profiles. A villager
can understand a foreign full order and an omitted-copula construction while
continuing to speak its own full native order. Foreign hearing no longer changes
an established native order merely by outnumbering native observations.

A profile groups actually heard phonetic word-token cues and learned role
patterns. It contains no speaker UUID, biome identifier, private vocabulary map,
intended clause or intended word order. Cues use the listener's own meanings for
those sounds. Public co-occurrence can link multiple speakers' conventions without
requiring private knowledge of their cultural faction. Biomes still establish
founding vocabularies/production in the existing way; a decoder never receives
the speaker's biome to choose the right interpretation.

Learning requires a uniquely aligned, independently public event and known
word meanings. Three observations are needed before a new receptive pattern is
used. Unknown/private events cannot teach it. Full orders remain permutations
of subject/verb/object; noncanonical patterns retain the bounded existing
construction rules, including IS-only copula omission and limited repetition.
There is no semantic oracle supplying absent WANT, unfamiliar negation or hidden
referent identity.

Reception is limited to eight dialect profiles, 24 cues per profile and eight
patterns per profile, with evidence counts bounded at 1000. Low-evidence profiles
can be forgotten; ambiguous cue overlap does not silently merge languages. This
is a finite candidate search, not recursive unrestricted parsing or an LLM. No
population-scale performance result is claimed from the small native fixture.

Selecting an interpretation

The decoder considers learned readings associated with the heard cue words and
the listener's existing native patterns. It validates grammatical roles and
reference bindings, then compares valid candidates using lexical cue support.
A unique best meaning can be used; equally supported conflicting meanings remain
unresolved. Strongly shared vocabulary with reversed subject/object orders can
therefore become ambiguous instead of being resolved by hidden speaker intent.
An unfamiliar construction can still be confidently misunderstood before useful
contradictory evidence exists, as in the earlier fallible translation model.
Evidence confidence is a heuristic of syntax familiarity, not calibrated truth.

Native output is generated from native production evidence, independently of
foreign receptive exposure. Actually producing a public form can reinforce one's
own convention. Same-language surface variants may still be accommodated when
the words are preferred local words and the role sequence fits the existing
native order. This preserves bounded local innovation/spread, such as dropping
IS, without converting foreign comprehension into automatic cultural replacement.
An actually new first grounded full order can seed a previously uninitialized
learner; it does not overwrite an already established order.

Integration

Sentence decoding, player glossaries, public-event language learning and visible
naming introductions use the new readings. A learned foreign naming order can
bind a name only when the speaker/place is actually visible and the naming words
are understood. Profiles cannot provide an unfamiliar name, coordinates or a
private relationship. Tense, negation, lexical ambiguity and repeated-reference
consistency checks remain enforced.

Children do not inherit parents' adult receptive profiles. Their existing weak
production exposure and sparse vocabulary remain separate. Bootstrap now preserves
partial receptive data and avoids giving an uninitialized developing child an
adult founding grammar. Adult bootstrap seeds only missing production evidence
while retaining existing receptive/production records. Deliberate teaching and
new word-formation mechanisms are still outstanding, not implicitly completed by
these preservation changes.

Persistence and compatibility

Mind schema 16 and player glossary schema 5 save the new profiles. Old mind-schema
15 and glossary-schema 4 data starts with empty receptive profiles rather than
inventing foreign comprehension. Native words, prior production, references,
commitments, work records, identities and histories remain intact. Existing
legacy productive constructions are retained. Incomplete one-observation learning
survives disk reload. Unknown future schemas retain existing protection.
Frozen controls can interpret with their saved profiles while actual hearing
leaves learned language unchanged.

Verification

Automated checks cover two foreign grammars distinguished by actual word cues,
native production retention, generalization to a different verb/object, full and
compressed foreign forms, insufficient exposure, indistinguishable-order
ambiguity, unfamiliar names, receptive naming with public referents, bounded
profile memory/evidence, non-inheritance, partial-child bootstrap, exact disk
reload and both schema migrations. Existing same-language innovation checks still
require learned patterns to generate previously unused combinations. Earlier
single-pool tests now assert comprehension without native-order replacement;
identical lexical cues cannot claim role certainty that the signal does not supply.
