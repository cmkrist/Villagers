VERIFIED LANGUAGE FOUNDATION: WORD FORMATION, CHILD/ADULT TEACHING AND NATIVE VOICES
2026-11-11

The implementation and its limits

Villagers now have bounded productive word formation. Four authored semantic
combinations distinguish wooden/stone hoes and swords. Known material and tool
roots combine using a learned order and linker; stem length is at most four
letters, with at most four stored conventions and evidence capped at 1000. Three
unambiguous public observations are required before a rule is productive. Hearing
foreign morphology does not replace an established native production convention.
Unknown roots, ambiguous stem collisions and conflicting readings stay unresolved.
Established atomic vocabulary retains precedence, preserving existing meanings.
Old enum ordinals and generic HOE/SWORD item recognition remain intact.

Founding biome languages can differ in head/material order and linker. Adults
retain authored founding conventions when bootstrapping an old missing formation
field. Developing children receive no adult formation rules. Both receptive word
evidence and incomplete formation evidence persist; player glossaries can also
infer an unheard compound from independently acquired roots and rules. This is
composition over four known semantic categories, not invention of a new category.

TutorLife is part of the ordinary villager controller after urgent needs,
committed work and food-help priorities. Teachers display actual owned items using
a permitted conserved equipment swap. Observers receive all public material/tool
features, not the teacher's selected noun. Varying exemplars can disambiguate a
shared head. Lessons use a demonstration, then a spoken request on a later
200-tick attention turn. A learner independently decodes the raw request and
points at an actually visible matching object, then speaks its own answer.

The teacher uses its own decoded reply and the public pointing gesture. It never
reads a learner dictionary or receives the learner's inferred category as an
answer key. A reply can sound correct to the teacher while the pointed object is
wrong; that lesson is not confirmed. Teacher journals store only their own
observed demonstrations and confirmed answers, capped at 32 peer/topic records.
A child's independently understood YES can reinforce its own successful words.
No food, tool, adult status, work authority, relationship or private memory is
created by a lesson. Unsafe, hungry, tired or interrupted bodies can stop teaching.

Adults can request deliberate teaching too. A listener's own incomplete decoding
of a public demonstration records a temporary need for help. An audible nearby
repair request includes a visible questioning gesture, allowing a teacher to
respond before all words are shared. The request and actual response are public;
teachers do not inspect private vocabulary to decide whether an adult is ignorant.
Adult lesson requests/attention are transient; acquired words, incomplete grammar
and the teacher's observed history are saved. Deliberate adult scheduling currently
uses villager bodies. Pillagers can hear and learn from cross-species public
examples, but a complete pillager school/negotiation system is not claimed.

Native voices and reciprocal imitation

Actual symbolic utterances now schedule native VILLAGER_AMBIENT or
PILLAGER_AMBIENT sound pulses. Word strings affect pitch contours, and the body's
stable UUID affects pitch/rhythm. This supplies individual accents alongside
shared regional founding dialects and vocabulary changed by life. It does not
create an unrelated language for every individual or guarantee unique voices in
an arbitrarily large population. Managed pillagers get separate species founding
vocabularies without replacing an existing saved vocabulary or cultural origin.

The sound scheduler is bounded to 128 active voice records and 126 pending pulses
per body. Normal consecutive phrases queue. Overflow explicitly interrupts the
older queue and records that interruption. Silent speakers cannot emit language.
Illager physical danger/food priorities and existing urgent native camp alarms
remain separate from these elective utterances.

Voice memory stores only actual heard sound strings and coarse public voice type,
not a speaker's intentions, vocabulary meanings, hidden biome or grammar. Each
body can attempt to reproduce a heard other-species phrase. Early attempts use
authored articulation substitutions; practice can improve reproduction and borrow
some target sample timbre. The actual altered strings enter acquisition and
interpretation. Existing dictionaries may misunderstand them confidently, or
remain uncertain. Sound-only parroting supplies no grounded meaning evidence,
although a listener can form a fallible attributed belief from an independently
understood utterance. Practice does not translate a phrase or grant its meanings.

The current audio is timed native grunts accompanying symbolic word strings.
It is not waveform recognition, reversible phonetic synthesis or a verified
natural-sounding spoken language. NPC semantic turns are currently atomic while
sound playback is timed. Client listening quality, turn synchronization under
crowded dialogue and population-scale audio mixing remain unmeasured.

Persistence

Mind schema 18 adds saved lesson histories and voice exemplars/practice; glossary
schema 6 adds formation evidence. Schema 16/17 minds and schema 5 glossaries
migrate without fabricating lesson success or heard voices. Existing identities,
words, references, beliefs, commitments and work records are retained. Children
start with sparse FIRST_WORDS and weak production exposure, without parents'
adult receptive/morphological mastery or private memories. Frozen controls use
known language while new lessons, imitation practice and hearing do not mutate
learned state. Unknown future-schema protections remain active.
