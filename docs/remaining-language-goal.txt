1. Grounded references and testimony

Villagers learn to identify particular people and places through actual encounters,
introductions and shared attention. References must be spoken and decoded using
the listener's knowledge, not recovered from a hidden speaker UUID or operator
coordinates. Unknown, ambiguous and mistaken references remain possible.

Acceptance: a speaker refers to a specific previously encountered person/place;
a listener independently resolves it, or asks for clarification. Hearing a generic
word cannot unlock a named person's reputation or exact private episode. Tests
must distinguish identity confusion, unfamiliar referents and valid testimony.

2. Source-aware reasoning and practical questions

Extend attributed beliefs into bounded reasoning about practical situations.
Villagers can ask follow-up questions, distinguish what they witnessed from what
someone told them, investigate uncertain claims and revise a conclusion after
contradictory evidence. Evidence and uncertainty travel with derived conclusions.

Acceptance: an interpreted answer changes an actual permitted decision; physical
investigation confirms or changes the belief. Repeated or circular gossip cannot
manufacture independent corroboration. Unknown facts are not supplied by world
omniscience, peer mind access or remote inventory inspection.

3. Persistent courtship and partnership conversations

Connect interpreted relationship dialogue to independent willingness, compatibility,
existing commitments and practical circumstances. Distinguish friendship,
partnership and parenthood. Preserve identities and existing family history.

Acceptance: mutual understood proposals can establish a persistent relationship;
refusal or misunderstanding cannot do so. Save/reload preserves the agreement.
Actual shared activities affect the relationship. Safety, resource requirements,
age and existing consent/capability rules remain authoritative. Partnership does
not automatically cause a birth or create food, beds or replacement villagers.

4. Work agreements and practical promises

Villagers negotiate concrete work with participants, tasks, terms and obligations.
An accepted agreement can guide existing permitted work, subject to actual skill,
tools, stock, reach, navigation and safety. Record completion, interruption and
unfulfilled obligations without automatically treating failure as intentional lying.

Acceptance: both parties decode the terms independently; a real action and
conserved resource transfer fulfill the agreement. Incomplete comprehension,
unwillingness, missing materials or failed execution do not award completion.
Persistent agreements survive restart and are revalidated against current facts.

5. Receptive grammar, word formation and childhood teaching

Maintain knowledge of foreign grammar separately from preferred native speech.
Support bounded formation/acquisition of new expressions and deliberate teaching
through observable examples and communicative outcomes. Children accumulate
knowledge through life rather than receiving adult mastery or inherited memories.

Acceptance: a bilingual speaker understands two constructions while retaining its
own production preference; an unfamiliar expression gains meaning through evidence;
a child learns beyond its starting vocabulary. Save/reload preserves incomplete
learning, and unrelated words/constructions are not silently granted.

6. Cultural transmission and grammar innovation evaluation

Use controlled populations, contact networks and generations to measure whether
learned conventions spread, persist, diverge and support useful communication.
Separate seeded conventions, supported innovations and genuinely novel discoveries.

Acceptance: retain conditions, random seeds, initial knowledge, interventions and
observed outcomes. Include suitable fixed or unexposed controls. Report failures
and ambiguity. Unrestricted invention of semantic or grammatical categories remains
an open research objective; a constrained construction experiment cannot prove it.

Shared boundaries

No LLM may bypass speech production, interpretation or learning. Each participant
acts on its own observations, beliefs and decoded language. Preserve saved words,
cultural origins, identities, existing populations and future-schema protection.
New words or agreements grant no new physical capability or resources. Survival
can interrupt elective social/work commitments. Frozen controls retain their
learned checkpoint while still using existing knowledge.

Verification and completion

For each milestone, implement meaningful automated checks and an appropriate
isolated native Minecraft experiment with pinned artifacts, real action receipts,
original-body preservation and restart verification. Never advance an existing
society world merely to prove a language feature. Record limitations, including
the distinction between server integration, visual UX, scaling and natural survival.

Keep this goal active while required implementation or verification remains.
Only mark it complete when the above engineering milestones and the explicitly
bounded cultural/innovation evaluation are satisfied. Do not describe unrestricted
grammar invention or human-like understanding as achieved without separate evidence.
